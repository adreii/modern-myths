# Modern Myths: the format

> Absurd little worlds that reveal the myths we live by.
> Each Short takes one essay from Roland Barthes' *Mythologies* (1957), turns its
> idea into a single impossible rule, and tells a small, warm story inside it.

## The formula (60–90 seconds, vertical)

Modelled on the "In this world…" story style: a deadpan absurd premise, a
child-sized narrator, an emotional turn, and a twist that reframes the rule.

| Beat | Time | What happens | Example (ep01 "Toys") |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Rule** | 0–3s | "In this world, ___." One impossible sentence. This is the hook. | *In this world, toys play themselves.* |
| **2. Escalation** | 3–20s | 3 quick, increasingly absurd examples. Each one gets a laugh. Rule of three, with the third one going furthest. | Kitchens cook → dolls change their own diapers → cars sit in traffic and honk |
| **3. The Stake** | 20–35s | The narrator gets personal. Someone they love, or something they want. | Grandpa gives me plain wooden blocks |
| **4. The Resistance** | 35–50s | The narrator rejects it, tries to fight it, or misunderstands it. | "They're broken." I hide them under my bed. |
| **5. The Turn** | 50–70s | Something happens that the rule didn't predict. | Bored at night, I build a tower… with sharks in top hats |
| **6. The Reveal** | 70–85s | "So now I know." One sentence that flips the rule into a truth. | *Some toys play for you. Some toys wait for you to show up.* |
| **7. The Card** | 4s | Silent lesson card, with credit to the essay. | *A toy that does everything makes you a user…* |

## Writing rules

1. **Never explain Barthes in the story.** The world *is* the explanation. The card at the end is the only place he's named.
2. **Use a first-person, child-sized narrator** ("kiddo" energy). Barthes is about seeing the obvious freshly, and children do that.
3. **Absurdity is literal.** Take the myth's metaphor and make it physically true. ("Toys prepare kids for adult life" → toys literally *do* adult life.)
4. **Make the jokes specific.** "Sharks wearing hats" beats "animals". "Does its own taxes" beats "works".
5. **Keep the lesson to one sentence a 10-year-old could repeat.**
6. **Barthes is copyrighted (1957).** Paraphrase only, and credit him on the card: *after Roland Barthes, "Toys", Mythologies (1957)*.
7. **Keep it short.** Aim for about 200–230 words, which is 75–90s with text-to-speech. Short sentences, and lots of full stops so the voice breathes.

## Visual bible

**Current style: `brick` (Roblox-like).** Blocky R6-style avatars, smooth saturated
plastic, game-style floors. The episode's `script.yaml` has `style: brick`, and real models
exported from Roblox Studio go in `cast.yaml`. Only use your own avatar, things you build
yourself, and assets you're licensed to use, and keep Roblox logos and names out of titles
and thumbnails so the channel doesn't look like an official Roblox channel.

The earlier `toy` style (wooden peg dolls), still available with `style: toy`:

- **World:** everyone and everything is a wooden toy. Characters are **peg dolls**
  (a cylinder body, a ball head, dot eyes and pink cheeks). This makes every episode
  feel like the same storybook world, and it's cheap to build in Python.
- **Look:** a soft diorama, like a toy-room corner set with warm key light, a pastel
  wall and a wooden floor. Each shot is a small stage.
- **Motion:** stop-motion feel at **12 fps** (rendered "on twos"). This is a style
  choice, and it also halves render time.
- **Palette:** cream walls, honey wood, tomato red, mustard, teal, and a night blue for sad beats.
- **Captions:** bold, centred lower third, 2–4 words at a time.
- **Card:** serif text on a dark background, with a 4-second hold.
